"From Translating Civilizations
to Translating the Self"

A Digital Humanities Project on Translation, Identity, and Cultural Memory

This project investigates the act of translation as a site of identity negotiation and cultural memory formation — examining how Arabic literary and testimonial texts travel into German, and what these crossings reveal about diaspora experience, belonging, and self-representation. Moving between the grand rhetoric of translating entire cultural civilizations and the intimate, fractured labor of individual translators navigating personal histories, the project traces how translated works shape — and are shaped by — questions of who speaks, for whom, and across which divide.

Drawing on Digital Humanities methods including corpus analysis, network visualization of translation flows, and structured annotation of paratextual materials, the project builds a digital layer over a fundamentally humanistic inquiry: what does it mean to translate a self across languages, cultures, and the experience of displacement?
