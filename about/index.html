Academic Background

I am a Master's student in Arabic Studies and Digital Humanities at Freie Universität Berlin, one of Germany's leading research universities. My academic journey is driven by a deep interest in how language functions as a site of identity construction, cultural memory, and social negotiation — particularly for Arabic-speaking communities in diaspora and migration contexts.

My interdisciplinary training brings together classical and contemporary Arabic linguistics, discourse analysis, and the computational methods of Digital Humanities. I am particularly interested in how digital tools — from text mining and corpus linguistics to interactive archives and data visualization — can illuminate patterns in language use, translation, and cultural production that conventional close reading cannot easily access.

Research Focus

At the core of my scholarly work lies the question of how multilingual individuals and communities maintain, negotiate, and transform their linguistic and cultural identities across borders. Drawing on discourse analysis, sociolinguistics, and postcolonial theory, I examine Arabic media texts, diasporic literature, and translation practices as arenas in which questions of belonging, authenticity, and hybridity are actively contested.

My current research engages with the Syrian diaspora experience in Germany — exploring how language choice, code-switching, and narrative construction reflect and shape processes of integration, memory, and collective identity. I approach these questions with both qualitative depth and an openness to digital methodologies, including computational text analysis and digital archive creation.

Intercultural Perspective

My own multilingual background — spanning Arabic, German, English, and Turkish — is not merely a biographical detail but a methodological resource. Having navigated multiple linguistic and cultural worlds, I bring a nuanced, embodied understanding of the dynamics I study. This perspective informs my commitment to scholarship that is both rigorous and attentive to the lived experiences of multilingual subjects.

Beyond research, I am engaged with the university community as a student assistant and mentor, contributing to both academic administration and the support of peers navigating the demands of higher education in a second or third language.
